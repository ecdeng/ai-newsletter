# House style research: executive-facing weekly AI newsletters (2026-10-29)

Method: one recent issue of each newsletter was read where paywalls allowed, plus the style guides in section 3. Axios blocked fetches, so Smart Brevity conventions come from secondary summaries.

**Works for a busy executive:** Import AI's labelled "Why this matters / Read more" pair; Brainfood's number-dense sentence headlines and thesis-first takes; Evident's fixed four-slot taxonomy; Pragmatic Engineer's contents block; Ben Evans's fact → comment → source rhythm; Zvi's bold BLUF sentence.
**Fails for them:** Zvi's length; Morning Brew's pun headlines that hide the news; event promotion mixed into news; narrative leads (great reading, poor skimming).

## 2. Recurring conventions worth encoding

1. **Headline carries the news, with a number.** "Amex fined $350m after $13bn of suspected laundering ran through its cards for 11 years." A headline must survive as the only line read: actor, action, one figure.
2. **Lead with the bottom line in one bold sentence.** First sentence states what happened; the second says what else is in the issue.
3. **Label the "so what" and keep it to ~60 words.** It must not repeat the lede.
4. **One primary source per item, on a labelled line.** Link the Federal Register PDF, not the news story about it.
5. **Thesis-first takes.** Bolded thesis sentence, then support.
6. **Separate fact, claim and opinion with verbs.** Confirmed = plain declarative; reported = "X reported/said"; rumour = "rumoured"/"unconfirmed" plus what would confirm it.
7. **Numbers are comparisons, not decoration.** "about 30x revenue", "nearly 12x what Brex sold for".
8. **Signal confidence in words, and flag thin data.** "Lots of very round numbers in this."
9. **Contents block up top.**
10. **Parallel, declarative section headers.** No puns.
11. **Fixed slots every week** so readers can jump.
12. **"What to watch" = dated, checkable items.** Event, date or window, and the decision it informs.

## 3. Style-guide rules (from guides read)

- **GOV.UK Clear language:** split sentences over 25 words; paragraphs ≤5 sentences; active voice; short words; no buzzwords; explain specialist terms on first use. At 14 words readers understand >90%; at 43 words, <10%. Most people read ~25% of a page.
- **Economist / Orwell:** never a long word where a short one will do; cut every word you can; active over passive; no jargon with an everyday equivalent; do not be stuffy or didactic.
- **Smart Brevity:** tease ≤6 words; lede direct and memorable; context follows the lede and never repeats it; one-syllable words and concrete nouns; avoid reflexive "could/might/may" (we modify: hedge once, precisely); the whole unit fits one phone screen.
- **Minto / Pyramid Principle:** answer first, then grouped support; SCQA openings; ≤3 groups.
- **Amazon narrative memos:** narrative forces knowing what matters more; metrics up front.

**Consolidated rules:** (1) answer first; (2) sentences ≤25 words, paragraphs ≤5 sentences; (3) active voice; (4) short concrete words; (5) no hype words (revolutionary, game-changing, unprecedented) unless quoting; (6) context after the lede, never repeating it; (7) label the so-what; (8) one idea per paragraph; (9) explain specialist terms on first use; (10) numbers as comparisons; (11) hedge once, precisely; (12) fixed slots and parallel headers; (13) cite the primary document.

## 4. Recommended template (1,500-2,500 words)

1. **Subject/headline** (≤10 words, biggest fact + number).
2. **The week in one paragraph** (≤80 words, bold first sentence).
3. **Three takeaways** (3 bullets, ≤30 words each, each with a verb aimed at the reader: "expect", "ask your vendor", "budget for"). Decisions, not summaries.
4. **Big stories** (2-3 × 200-300 words): bold sentence-headline → what happened (2-3 sentences, dated, attributed) → **Why it matters** (≤60 words) → **Our read** (confidence stated) → **Source:** primary link.
5. **Deals and launches** (5-10 one-liners: actor, action, number, source; take only where non-obvious).
6. **Regulation and policy** (2-4 items; mark each *law / proposal / guidance / enforcement* with effective date).
7. **People and moves** (3-6 lines: name, from → to, why it signals anything).
8. **What to watch** (3-5 dated items: event, date/window, what it would change).
9. **Sources** (numbered primary documents, one-line note each).

**Slow week:** one big story, fold deals/policy/moves into one "Also this week" list ≤8 lines, keep takeaways and watch list, target 1,200 words.
**Busy week:** add a fourth big story before adding words elsewhere, lengthen "Our read" with the second-order question, add a "Numbers" box, cap at 2,500 and move overflow to sources.

## 5. Before/after rewrites

**Model launch — before:** "This week saw the exciting release of a groundbreaking new model from a major lab, which the company says delivers unprecedented performance across a wide range of legal and financial tasks and is expected to transform how professionals work."

**After:** "**Lab X shipped Model Y on Tuesday; it scores 58.3% on an attorney-defined contract benchmark, 0.2 points above the previous leader.** Pricing is unchanged. **Why it matters:** a sub-60% pass rate on weighted attorney criteria means review still needs a lawyer in the loop; the gain is cost, not accuracy. **Our read:** the number is the vendor's own; treat it as a floor for comparison, not a verdict, until the benchmark publisher's run lands next week. **Source:** Lab X launch post; benchmark methodology PDF."

**Law-firm adoption — before:** "A leading global law firm has announced a major partnership with an AI company to roll out generative AI tools across the firm, in a move that highlights the growing importance of AI in the legal industry."

**After:** "**Firm A will deploy Vendor B's contract tool to all 2,400 lawyers by Q1, per the firm's press release; terms undisclosed.** It is the third AmLaw 20 firm on Vendor B this quarter. **Why it matters:** firm-wide, mandatory rollouts shift the buyer from innovation teams to practice heads, which changes who vendors must sell to. **Unknown:** whether billing-rate changes follow; the release does not say. **Watch:** Firm A's partner-profit figures in February."

**Bank deployment — before:** "Bank C has implemented an AI assistant for its employees, which it says is helping staff be more productive and is part of its broader digital transformation strategy with significant investment in AI."

**After:** "**Bank C put an internal LLM assistant in front of 140,000 staff, up from a 2025 pilot of 20,000, according to its Q3 earnings call.** The CFO cited no dollar savings. **Why it matters:** this is the largest disclosed employee rollout at a G-SIB; peers now have a comparable to be asked about on their own calls. **Our read:** productivity claims without a savings line are marketing until a cost ratio moves; Bank C's efficiency ratio is the number to check next quarter. **Source:** Q3 earnings transcript, p. 7."
